02 / How it works

The complete
qualification journey.

Every stage exists for one reason: by the time the work reaches your team, they know what the problem is, where it is, who is responsible, whether they can get in, what to bring, and what needs to happen next.

01
Recognise

Find the real customer,
not the inbox noise.

Real work can be hidden inside a platform notification, a forwarded email, a property manager’s message, a web form, or a direct chat.

Hermia recognises the genuine request for help and keeps the customer’s details connected to it. Marketing, receipts, supplier noise, and irrelevant messages do not get treated like jobs just because they landed in the same inbox.

Outcome / a cleaner front door
02
Clarify

Turn everyday language
into a useful job description.

Customers do not write like technicians. They explain the problem in their own words, often messy, incomplete, or emotional.

Hermia establishes what is actually wrong and what is affected. A leak, blockage, hot-water issue, burst pipe, switchboard fault, roof problem, alarm issue, or remedial defect becomes a clear professional summary your team can understand quickly.

Outcome / a clearer starting point
03
Confirm

Make sure the site is
real, reachable, and relevant.

A job should not quietly land in the wrong suburb, state, country, or outside the business’s actual service area.

Hermia gathers and confirms the complete site context: full address, suburb, state, country, building type, and the information that makes the location actionable. It is more than a street line because the technician is the one travelling there.

Outcome / a real site, not a vague location
04
Prepare

Understand access, safety,
and the conditions on site.

Getting to the address is not the same as being able to attend the work safely and professionally.

Hermia asks about access and conditions before arrival: whether somebody will be home, if a property is locked or restricted, whether a key, code, gate, reception sign-in, or induction is needed, and whether there are hazards or conditions the crew should know about.

Outcome / fewer surprises at the door
05
Make it trade-specific

Ask what the work
actually requires.

Different trades have different readiness questions. A generic checklist cannot tell a technician what they truly need to know.

Hermia follows the questions that matter for the job: water or gas isolation for plumbing, switchboard isolation for electrical, safe roof access for roofing, site restrictions for remedial work, or monitoring and notification context for fire and essential-safety work.

Outcome / preparation that fits the trade
06
Plan

Put timing, materials,
and commercial context in view.

“Sometime this week” is not a plan. Neither is leaving the office to discover who pays, what is supplied, or whether a customer is comfortable proceeding.

Hermia records a clear preferred time, materials position, residential or commercial context, billing contact, and estimate context. Timing is shaped around the business’s real capacity, so the operation is not making promises that create a conflict later. Where the business provides an estimate, Hermia can present it clearly; if the customer needs a commercial discussion, it brings your team into that decision rather than guessing on their behalf.

Outcome / a next move the operation can use
07
Protect quality

Do not guess. Do not send
a half-finished job forward.

A vague answer is not the same as useful information, and a non-answer should not become an assumption.

When the customer is unclear, Hermia asks again in plain language. Photos and video remain tied to the same story. Repeat contact does not automatically create another loose record. The handover stays grounded in the detail the customer actually provided.

Outcome / less rework, less chasing

07 / Engineered like infrastructure

Not a chatbot
with a single-trade prompt.

The difference between Hermia and a generic assistant is not how well it writes. It is what the system is allowed to decide—and what it is never allowed to guess.

The decision
layer.

Before a response is sent, the customer’s answer is assessed against fixed checks for that exact question and that exact trade. Does the address include the detail needed to reach the site? Does “pipe” identify which pipe? Does “power” identify which circuit? Does the fault description give a crew something real to act on?

The same input receives the same verdict every time—late at night, during a busy period, or on the ten-thousandth job. Hermia’s language is warm and human, but the decision about whether an answer is complete does not drift with the wording of the conversation.

That separation matters: Hermia uses its intelligence to explain the next question clearly, not to invent an answer or quietly waive a standard.

Check. Decide. Explain.

Every customer response moves through the same practical sequence before it becomes part of a job brief.

01Check the detailIs the answer specific enough for this job, this question, and this trade?
02Keep the standardIf the detail is missing, it stays missing; it is not filled in on the customer’s behalf.
03Explain the next moveThe customer receives a clear, trade-appropriate question that helps them provide what the team needs.
01 / PRICE CONSISTENCY

No invented prices.

Where an estimate applies, Hermia starts with the pricing PDF or approved price list your business provides. It then considers the affected asset type and the fault detail the customer has actually supplied to find the closest supported estimate. It remains an estimate, never a final price. If the available pricing or job detail does not support a confident match, it is surfaced to your team instead of guessed.

02 / QUALITY CONTROL

No accidental rejections.

A useful answer is not pushed back because the language is informal. The standard is about whether the detail is workable—not whether the customer speaks like a technician.

03 / FACTS ONLY

No made-up details.

Nothing is inferred, guessed, or filled in on the customer’s behalf. If it was not said or shown, it does not become part of the job story.

04 / COMPLETE BEFORE HANDOVER

Nothing half-finished gets through.

Vague answers are followed up with clear options that make it easy for the customer to respond. The job does not move forward as if uncertainty were detail.

05 / HUMAN WHEN NEEDED

Uncertainty stays visible.

When a price, job, or customer answer cannot be handled with confidence, it is surfaced for the business rather than quietly turned into an assumption.

06 / URGENCY STAYS WATCHED

Critical changes reopen attention.

If a later message introduces emergency, urgent, or critical language, the conversation receives fresh attention instead of remaining closed while a genuine change waits.
